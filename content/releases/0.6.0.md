Every drawing set arrives with its own conventions. One consultant sends titles
in screaming caps, the next sends them in sentence case. A sheet printed `S · 10`
comes back from OCR as `S - 10`. Nectar read all of those correctly — they just
weren't in *your* house style, and fixing them one row at a time is the kind of
work Nectar exists to remove.

**Data Rules** applies your formatting to the whole set at once. Open the rail
from **Data Rules** in the toolbar, set the shape you want, and every file in the
session follows it — including the ones you add tomorrow.

## What you can set

- **Case** — upper, lower, title, or sentence, set separately for sheet number
  and sheet title.
- **Separator** — normalize the punctuation between a sheet number's parts.
  `A 101`, `A.101`, and `A - 101` all land on the one you chose.
- **Find & replace** — for anything genuinely bespoke, with optional regex, scoped
  to either field or both.

## Nothing is overwritten

Rules are a lens, not an edit. Nectar always stores the raw extracted value and
applies your rules on the way out — to the table, the output name, the rename, and
the export. Switch the master toggle off and every file reverts to exactly what
was read off the sheet. There is never a question of which value is the real one.

That also means re-extraction can't fight your rules, and new files inherit them
automatically. There's no "re-apply" step to forget.

## When a rule is right about the set and wrong about one sheet

It happens. A rule that fixes ninety-four sheets mangles the ninety-fifth.
Editing that value by hand doesn't help, because the rule fires again on whatever
you type.

So you can **pin** a single field on a single file. The pin tells the lens to
look away for that one value and leave what you typed alone. Everything else in
the set keeps following the rule.
